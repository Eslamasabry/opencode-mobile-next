# OpenCode Mobile 3.0.0+10070

**The first time you open the app after updating, it refreshes the OpenCode that runs on your phone (about 130 MB) and, if you use coding agents on your phone, their helper (about 330 MB). Both wait for Wi-Fi. Everything keeps working meanwhile.**

This is the first native Android release of OpenCode Mobile for everyone. It installs over the old app (2.1.5) and over the earlier 3.0.0 pre-release (build 10020), in place, and keeps your data. The long lists below say what is new, what is better and what was fixed, area by area. If you only want the headlines, read "In short" and the section that matches the version you are coming from.

## In short

- **A new app, same place.** OpenCode Mobile is now a native Android app. It updates the old app in place: your servers, drafts, settings and the phone's own Linux stay.
- **A turn's work is one list of steps.** Commands, reads, edits and sub-agents read as plain words, fold away when they went well, and stay open where something failed or waits for you.
- **Agents on your phone.** Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity and OpenCode run on the phone itself, with their sign-in state, usage limits and updates shown in plain words. They update themselves while nothing is working.
- **New conversation says where it will run** and who will work on it, and it opens on your last setup.
- **Phone storage for the phone's agents.** Choose a folder in Android's shared storage and the agents can work in it, with Git.
- **Home is the front door.** Recent conversations from every connection, filters, a search that covers the phone's agents, and a quiet line that says what the phone is getting ready.
- **Answer from anywhere.** Approve or decline a request on its step, from its Home row or from the edge light, with Undo.
- **Scheduled prompts** run on the server you chose, say when they may start late, and keep running after the update from the old app (for computers).
- **Look and feel.** Six styles (Solid is the default; Glass is one you can choose), twenty live backgrounds (off by default), one text-size rule, and a screen that stays upright on phones.
- **Nine languages.** English, Arabic, Spanish, French, Indonesian, Portuguese, Russian, Vietnamese and Simplified Chinese.
- **Honest privacy wording.** Settings › Privacy and data lists every event and field the optional reports can send, and the sharing question waits until after your first finished reply.
- **Fewer crashes and stalls.** A long list of start-up, update, layout and memory problems found on real phones is fixed.

## Coming from 2.1.5

The old app (2.1.5) is a different, older app built with another toolkit. This release replaces it in place. You do not uninstall anything and you do not sign in to the app again.

**What is kept**

- **Servers and their passwords.** Saved servers are carried over, including This phone, and passwords stored by the old app are moved into the new app's safe storage, not left behind.
- **Settings and look.** The new app reads the old app's saved settings in place, so your theme, style, background and other choices are there on first open. A language you chose in the old app is carried over when the phone's own language setting does not already hold one (Japanese is not offered yet, so that choice is not carried over).
- **Drafts.** Unsent drafts are read from the same saved list the old app wrote.
- **Pinned conversations and launcher shortcuts.** Pins are kept, and pinned conversation shortcuts on your launcher are published again under the same names, so they still open the right conversation.
- **Conversations.** Your conversations live on your servers and come back from them; the choices you made per conversation (such as its model and agent) are kept with the saved settings.
- **Scheduled prompts** made for a computer are armed again on first open, so they keep running without being saved again.
- **Widgets and quick tiles** are asked to redraw, and notification channels keep the importance you set in Android.
- **Background connection.** If you had the app keeping its connection alive in the background, it is resumed after the update (an Android pause, such as the Android 15 six-hour limit, is never overridden).
- **This phone's server** that the old app left running is brought back by itself, and the phone's Linux is reused: there is no new setup screen and no new Linux download.

**What happens on first open**

- The app goes straight to Conversations, in the look you had. No sign-in, password, consent, setup or tour is asked again.
- The phone refreshes its OpenCode (about 130 MB) and, if you use agents on the phone, their helper (about 330 MB). Both downloads wait for Wi-Fi, run silently and show no progress yet. Until the refresh is done, conversations keep working on the OpenCode you had.
- If you use coding agents on the phone, the agents' helper starts by itself when you open the app, and the installed agents move to the versions this app ships with while no agent is working.
- About a minute in, one sheet may appear once: "Keep the server running?" with "Allow background running" and "Not now". It only asks; saying "Not now" changes nothing else.

**What to do once**

- **A computer that still runs OpenCode 1 has to be updated to OpenCode 2.** This app no longer supports OpenCode 1; it says so on that server and nothing on the computer is changed by the app.
- A "This phone" scheduled prompt made in the old app has to be opened and saved once before it runs again. Schedules for a computer need nothing.
- If an agent shows as signed out after updating, sign in again from its page.

**Things that are different from the old app**

- The app is for 64-bit phones only.
- Japanese is not offered yet; the app reads English in that case.
- Phones stay in portrait on every screen; tablets and unfolded foldables rotate.
- Other running conversations are found through Rings or the edge light, as you choose in Settings (see Notifications, widget and edge light).
- Gemini CLI is no longer offered for new installs; a copy you already have is kept.
- A server that the old app set up with its own Termux setup is kept as a server you run yourself; the new app never starts it, and when it is off Home treats it as off, not as broken (see Home).

### Conversations: reading, sending, steering and approvals

**New**

- **A turn's work is one list of steps.** The old summary work line and the separate Thought fold are replaced by one step list on a rail: a turn that ended well is one row that opens in place, a running turn shows its newest rows, and a failed, waiting or stopped turn stays open at that step. Older rows fold behind one row that names what was declined or failed. Sub-agents that ran together are one group.
- **Steps read as plain words.** A command is named by what it ran after its set-up (tests, a build, a search, a read, git) instead of the same "go to the project folder and load the toolchain" start every row shared. Commands one after another fold into one run, each kind of step has its own larger glyph, tools are named in words, and the agent's words between steps are whole, never their first streamed piece, and quieter than the steps.
- **Steps arrive in one movement.** The fold row ("Show 2 earlier steps") comes and goes at the same pace as the arriving step, the rows that fold behind it move with it, steps open like an accordion without a jump, an opened run's steps stand inside it, and a row you tapped stays under your finger even in a short conversation.
- **Answer a waiting permission under its step.** The approval card shows the command, or a preview of four lines of a change with "Show all", and names its file ("Edit this file?" now names it, as "Create ..." did). Allow once and Deny sit side by side, "Always allow" shows its scope in plain words above the technical fold, and one pair of answers is used on the card, its Details, the Home rows and the notification.
- **Deny denies at once.** The note you can add lives in Details, and a note sent with a Deny reaches the model as your words. The step reads "declined" immediately, and Allow stops it waiting immediately.
- **A shared Undo window** for permission answers, wherever you answered, and Stop in a running row's menu.
- **Running work is always findable.** The conversation's menu lists tasks, commands and sub-agents (including Claude Code's background tasks, which now count as background work) and says in words what a server cannot do. Send to background now works on OpenCode 2.
- **Find marks every match** in the transcript, brings the current one clear of the bars, shows its position, and says "Searching..." until the matches arrive. Find is one row above the message box, the phrase chips fold while it is open, and one Back closes it.
- **Cost and tokens.** The context sheet and Details show what the conversation cost. "Show time, tokens and cost" is a setting, and message timestamps can be shown or hidden from the conversation's commands.
- **Sub-agents.** A sub-agent's conversation names its parent ("Parent › sub-agent") and goes back to it without stacking it twice. Go to › Subagents is offered only where the server can list them, and it now works on OpenCode 2 (forks are left out).
- **Continue with another agent** from any conversation, with a plain folder check first. "Continue in a new conversation" on a conversation its agent cannot take up again opens a new conversation with the same agent in the same folder, with a short hand-over of what was done in the box.
- **Open on another phone** draws a QR code of the link with Copy and offers the server-address switch when the host allows it.
- **Attach or save a file the agent produced** when it comes as inline content: a small sheet offers Attach to prompt and Save.
- **A question the agent asks can be dismissed**; stop-after-step can be undone; a steer note (a message the agent reads when its step finishes) shows a Stop after eight seconds and, once stopped, the waiting words go out again.
- **A request that is still waiting stays in view**: a message you send meanwhile never takes it out of view, and a waiting permission card retires the moment Stop is pressed instead of staying for seconds.
- **Hardware keyboard keys** for a conversation: Find, F3 and F2, background, terminal and launcher, a Ctrl+K in the shell, and a help page that tells the truth about them.
- **A tool call the agent formed wrongly** (for example a form question with a missing field) reads as its own kind of failure, and a call you refused with a note reads as declined, not failed.
- **A conversation says when a reply was cut off or lost** ("The connection dropped before this reply finished"), with Send again or Continue. A retrying turn says why in plain words, such as the provider not answering, and offers another model.

### Long conversations and speed

**Better**

- A streaming reply costs the same after 20 turns or 1,000. The transcript list redoes only the live turn, groups turns again only from the first changed one, and makes Find's text once per settled turn, not on every publish.
- Failed-turn limit offers walk the live turn and the failed turns, not every row for every footer.
- Scrolling a conversation or Home no longer redraws page-sized layers every frame, and the page's state changes only when something in it changed.
- New conversation shows the setup last read in this run at once and checks it again behind, instead of waiting five to eight round trips on every visit.
- Phone agents' model lists are read ahead and kept across starts, without starting the helper; New conversation shows the last list at once and says "loading" instead of "no models" while it reads.
- A phone agent's conversation keeps its saved turns across turns, the helper's transcript shelf is sized from the number of phone-agent conversations, and the top ten Home rows are warmed into memory once.
- A phone agent's conversation opened before opens to read at once; the first message to a phone agent waits only for that agent's own start, not for every other agent still loading.

- Code blocks take an offscreen layer only while they clear something, and page edges over a flat ground draw no offscreen layer.
- The launch window shows the app's ground and mark, then an "Opening" line after two seconds.

### Agents on the phone

**New**

- **Latest versions, updated by themselves.** Installed agents and their helper move to the versions this app ships with when nothing is working and the network is not metered. Claude Code now offers Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2; Codex, Goose, Oh My Pi, Qwen Code, Kilo, Antigravity and fx are on newer versions, and the helper itself is newer.
- **Updates cover every agent that is behind**, not only the first, and no longer wait for you to open the Agents page. A copy that differs from the pinned version, or has no record (such as Codex installed as the bare program), is replaced by itself.
- **Set up at first use.** Qwen Code, Goose and Oh My Pi (and an installed Gemini CLI) are usable at once and offer Set up from their card; their first answer tells whether they work. If the first answer says a model or key is missing, the conversation offers the agent's set-up.
- **Your own agent and provider.** Add your own agent, use your own provider with Claude Code, and add a provider endpoint the catalogue does not list. A "How this works" sheet explains it. A saved or removed own agent reaches the helper at once when it is idle.
- **Reading old conversations.** A saved Claude Code conversation on This phone opens again, a phone agent's conversation opens to read after a restart (the home row no longer says "Can't reopen"), and reading earlier conversations never starts their agents.
- **Check, restart, retry.** "Check <agent>" runs the phone check from the tile or sheet; a tile says when the helper is stopped; Restart agent is on a phone agent's connection line, and Retry starts a stopped helper.
- **Install help.** An install that failed or was interrupted stays on its tile with its reason and Try again; a running one names its step. The install review counts shared parts not yet on the phone and says no before starting when there is not enough room (a bad download reads as a bad download, and a full disk is not hidden by a leftover signal).
- **Antigravity** has its own mark. When it ends its turn with a command still running (it waits only about ten seconds), the app asks it once to wait and continue, so it no longer "disconnects between the terminal commands".
- **Codex** on the phone can run commands, installed as its official package so its command host is there, and it asks before every command and every file change under Ask first.
- **The phone's own OpenCode** takes providers you add from the Providers page.

**Better**

- **The waiting line names the real step.** While a phone agent (or This phone's own OpenCode) gets ready, the conversation's waiting line says "Starting Linux", "Starting OpenCode", "Starting the agents' helper", "Starting <agent>" or "Opening the conversation", with a turning mark and who it connects to, and the page crosses over to the conversation when it is ready. ## Reporting a problem

Open Settings › Help › About for the build number, then tell us what you tapped and what you saw. Settings › Report a problem keeps the recent errors and lets you share them. Say which screen you were on, whether a phone agent or a computer was involved, and whether it happened right after the update.
